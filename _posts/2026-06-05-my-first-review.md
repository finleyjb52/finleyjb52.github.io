### The Rollercoaster of Life

Most songs take a deep dive into one small part of the human emotion, whether it's love, or regret, or hurt, there's always something powerful about truly relating to a song. Sometimes, songs take a step back and look at life as a whole; this is exactly what Burn Burn Burn by Zach Bryan does.

On the face of it, I can't really relate to almost anything to do with Zach Bryan. He's a global superstar, I'm just a regular person. He's a maverick, he gets into trouble, he doesn't care what people think. I play it safe, I do what I'm told and (to quote Taylor Swift) I'm possibly a pathological people pleaser. And yet, when it all boils down, I think it would be hard to find someone who doesn't relate to the lyrics of the song.

He starts the song by saying 'Everyone seems a damn genius lately' and then 'Still so much I have yet to know'. This is the feeling of always being one step behind, your mind telling you that everyone else has it figured out except for you. He starts the song with constant juxtaposition just like this. Singing 'We get dressed up just to go downtown in some ego-filled late night crowd, it seems to be where I feel most alone'. By comparing getting 'dressed up' to go 'downtown' and being in a 'crowd' yet feeling 'alone', he echoes that feeling of not fitting in. You can feel his naive resentment towards those he feels don't get what it's like to feel lost as he calls them 'ego-filled'. In life you learn that you pretty much everyone else is feeling the same, they're just hiding it - just like you are.

It's in these moments that you look for comfort, nostalgia to a time when things were easier, and life didn't feel like one big competition. He yearns for 'a shade tree and a honey hole and to talk to my grandpa again'. He begins to search for answers to his emotion struggles, turning to God and nature, and yet none of it works, he sings 'I see God in everything, the trees and pain and nights in the spring, so why do I still long for a home?'.

As he can't seem to find the answers to his questions he recounts all the emotions he felt. He even suggests at times he experienced suicidal thoughts, asking to 'feel the fear of never waking'. He looks to destroy himself: 'I want to drown in rot gut whiskey', 'Find a bar and get in a fight' and escape it all 'Leave this small town for a while'. Despite this level of resentment towards himself and the world, he still maintains juxtaposition, but this time reminding himself of the good in the world: 'I'd like to love my lady long and hard', 'Head to Paris on a late night flight', 'Write a few poems on a sunny balcony'. I like to think of this as progress for Zach, he frees himself from his fixation on comparing himself to other people, and instead focuses on his own internal struggle to find meaning in life.

From this reflection, he gains purpose. He sings 'I know I'm bound to die one day so when I reach those golden gates I pray to say I did the best I can'. He accepts that he isn't perfect, but commits to doing the best he can in what's left of his life. Better yet, after coming to peace with his own purpose, he asks to be able to help others who feel the same to 'Let 'em know it all turns out just fine'.

He now takes a moment to reflect, he understands himself now, he is proud of who he is: 'I'm a simple man, I don't need much, just my simple songs and some human touch'. This is the final destination of his journey, he is done with trying to be like anyone else, he is happy in himself. After his path towards self destruction he sings 'I'm tired now, so I'm bringin' my ass home', acknowloging where his heart belongs.

The song doesn't follow a normal strucutre, it has no chorus, yet he chooses to repeat the outro three times. I think he chose this abnormal structure with no chorus to echo the idea that life isn't what you expect, every day and every line of the song you will experience something new, but by the end of it you will have found peace. 

In the outro he sings 'So let me go down the line, let me feel it all, joy, pain, and sky, so let me go down the line, we all burn, burn, burn and die'. Whilst the lyrics of the outro stay the same each time he sings it, for me, each of the 3 times have a different meaning, which reflect his journey. The first time he sings it normally, he's not quite sure what it means yet and he's learning how to feel about life. The second time he sings louder, with anger and fear, he now knows death will come one day but he resents it and faces denial. Finally, the third time he sings softly and at peace, his journey is complete and he is grateful for the rollercoaster that is life.
